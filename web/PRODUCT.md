# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Turkish holidaymakers and coast-dwellers, 25 to 45, planning or living a summer around the
Aegean and Mediterranean. They are on a laptop or phone at home in Istanbul, Ankara or Izmir in
late spring, working out whether the week in Cesme is going to end in a burn again. They are not
looking for a medical device and they are not looking for a beauty product. They want a number:
how long can I stay out today.

Secondary: English-reading visitors, mostly expatriates and tourists, arriving from an App Store
listing or a link. Same job, different language.

The job to be done is a purchase decision, made in under ninety seconds, on trust. There is no
free tier and no trial, so the site has to earn a one-time payment without a demo.

## Product Purpose

Bronzla calculates a safe sun exposure time from the live UV index, the user's Fitzpatrick skin
type and their SPF. It runs a timer with flip and reminder alerts, forecasts UV ten days ahead,
and logs sessions.

The site exists to convert a cold visitor into a paid download, and to host the privacy and
terms pages App Review requires. Success is a store tap. Nothing else on the site matters more
than that, and nothing on the site should get in its way.

## Brand Personality

Coastal, warm, easygoing. The voice of a friend who happens to know the science: relaxed about
the holiday, exact about the numbers. Turkish first, and Turkish in idiom rather than in
translation.

It is confident without being loud. It never sells fear, and it never sells a tan. The safety
rigour is present as evidence, not as a warning label: the WHO UV definition, published minimal
erythemal doses, sunscreen discounted fifty per cent for real-world use. Those facts are the
reason to trust it, so they are stated plainly and then left alone.

Privacy is a stance, not a feature bullet. No account, no analytics, nothing leaves the device,
bought once. Said flatly, without congratulating ourselves.

## Anti-references

- **Tanning and beauty brands.** No bronzer gold, no oiled skin, no aspirational swimwear
  photography, no sunset gradient washes. This is the explicit ban. A sun app that looks like a
  cosmetics brand destroys the safety framing it is selling.
- **Generic SaaS landing pages.** No gradient hero, no row of three identical cards with rounded
  icons above each heading, no logo cloud, no invented testimonial counts.
- **Sterile medical UI.** No clinical blue-and-white, no stethoscope stock photography. Credible
  is the goal; cold is not.
- **Editorial magazine affectation.** No display serif italic headline over ruled columns with
  small tracked mono labels. That is the current saturated lane and this is not a magazine.

## Design Principles

1. **The number is the hero.** Bronzla's whole value is a single figure: how long you can stay
   out. The site leads with that figure the way the app does, not with a slogan about summer.
2. **Shade, not bronze.** The visual world is the cool side of the sun: deep water, shadow, the
   inside of an awning. Warmth arrives through language and a single signal colour, never
   through a beige or gold surface.
3. **Colour carries meaning or it stays out.** The UV band scale is a real instrument from the
   app. It is the only decorative-looking thing permitted, because it is not decoration.
4. **Show the reasoning once, then stop.** Cite the sources, state the fifty per cent sunscreen
   discount, and move on. Trust is built by being specific once, not by repeating that we care.
5. **Nothing between the visitor and the store.** No modal, no newsletter interruption, no
   cookie banner (there is nothing to consent to, because nothing is tracked).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at or above 4.5:1, large text at or above 3:1, verified rather than
  assumed. The dark surface makes this easy to get wrong and easy to check.
- The UV scale must never rely on hue alone. It rises monotonically in luminance and always
  carries a numeral and a band name, matching the app's own rule for colour vision deficiency.
- Full `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives. Every reveal degrades to a crossfade or to nothing.
- Turkish typography is a hard requirement: dotted and dotless i, and the full Latin Extended-A
  set, must render correctly at every weight. A font that breaks on `İstanbul` or `Çeşme` is
  disqualified regardless of how it looks.
- Keyboard-reachable everything, visible focus rings, real landmarks and heading order.
